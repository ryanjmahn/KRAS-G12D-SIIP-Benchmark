# Phase 2 figures

**Figure P2-1. What predicts whether a target's prediction is any good — and why the obvious measurement says the wrong thing (n = 846 training targets).** For each tool, the AUC for separating its well-predicted targets from the rest, using each candidate confidence signal; bars are bootstrap 95% CIs over targets (2,000 resamples), uncorrected for the 15 comparisons shown. The top two signals are free, needing no ensemble: the tool's own highest apo score and the mean of its top-10. The lower three are measured over 10 perturbed conformers (random motion). **Filled:** targets split against one global median of apo AUPRC. **Hollow:** split within base-rate quintiles. The two disagree because per-target AUPRC has a floor at the pocket-residue base rate, which falls with protein size, so the filled markers credit or penalise signals for tracking size rather than prediction quality. Corrected, the probability tools' own confidence separates well from badly predicted targets at AUC 0.64 and 0.61, where the raw measurement put it near chance, while fpocket's own score and every perturbation signal stay there.

**Figure P2-2. Among a tool's top-10 residues, stability under perturbation adds a little to its ranking.** Mean per-target gain in AUC (true pocket residues ranked above false ones) over the tool's own apo score, for each tool and each of the three motion generators. (a) Stability alone: how often the residue stays in a conformer's top-10. (b) Stability rank-summed with the tool's own score, so nothing is fitted. Paired Wilcoxon over targets, Holm-corrected across all 18 rows, printed at the right of each row and greyed where p_holm ≥ 0.05; bars are bootstrap 95% CIs of the mean paired difference. Hollow: the held-out test split. Random motion matches or beats the normal modes everywhere, so the signal is not about dynamics. fpocket gains most because it has the least to lose: it scores a residue by the best pocket containing it, so its top-10 is one tied block on 84% of targets and it cannot rank inside its own shortlist at all.

**Figure P2-3. Withholding the least stable predictions buys precision only where the tool has no ranking of its own (n = 846 training targets).** Each target contributes its top-10 apo residues; at a given coverage every target keeps that same share of them, ranked within the target, so no target can crowd out another. Annotated: the gain of stability + own score over the own score alone at 20% coverage, with a paired cluster-bootstrap 95% CI over targets. fpocket's own curve is flat on its base rate because it cannot order its own shortlist; stability supplies the ordering. P2Rank and AE-PocketMiner already rank their shortlists, and gain nothing. Dashed: the held-out test split.
